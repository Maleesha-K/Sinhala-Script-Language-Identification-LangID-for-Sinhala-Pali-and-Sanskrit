Breaking the Orthographic Fallacy in Planetary-Scale Language Identification

A rigorous empirical framework disambiguating Sinhala, Pali, and Sanskrit within the shared Sinhala script, solving the catastrophic failure of planetary foundation models and uncovering morphological inflection advantages under extreme contextual deprivation.

📄 Paper Target: ACL / ARR Official Format
📊 Dataset Scale: 67,332 Target + 110,000 Multi-Lang
🔬 Models Evaluated: 7 Baselines + 6 SOTA Foundation
🌐 Benchmarks: FLORES+, CommonLID, WiLI-2018
"The Orthographic Fallacy is the implicit architectural assumption that Unicode script uniquely maps to a single language. In South Asia, the Sinhala script has historically inscribed Sinhala, canonical Pali, and classical Sanskrit. Consequently, planetary LangID systems (fastText LID-176, GlotLID v3, NLLB LID-218, OpenLID-v2) collapse to 0.00% recall on Sinhala-script Pali and Sanskrit, unilaterally misclassifying them as modern Sinhala."

Discovery of the Shared-Script Failure Mode

We systematically prove that stock planetary LangID systems suffer from complete zero-shot collapse (0.00% recall) on Sinhala-script Pali and Sanskrit. Because classification heads map Unicode blocks monotonically, billions of web pages and digitized historical manuscripts are erroneously funneled into modern Sinhala.

Empirical Proof: Stock GlotLID v3, NLLB LID-218, fastText LID-176, and OpenLID-v2 all yield 0.00% F1 on Pali-Sinh and San-Sinh across FLORES+, WiLI, and CommonLID.
02
🔬

Morphological Stress Testing & Subword Inductive Bias

In Phase 1, we prove that while full sentences saturate across all architectures (>0.994 F1), single-word stress testing creates an architectural chasm. Simple probabilistic character $n$-gram models drastically outperform deep neural networks and margin classifiers by capturing diagnostic inflectional affixes without requiring sentence syntax.

1-Word Stress Test: Multinomial NB (0.8584 F1) & fastText (0.8027 F1) beat Char-CNN (0.7654), Char-BiGRU (0.6916), and Linear SVM (0.6099).
03
🌐

Non-Contaminated Hybrid Benchmarking Taxonomy

We constructed three standardized 11-language hybrid benchmarks from FLORES+, CommonLID, and WiLI-2018. By replacing generic Sinhala text with 7,047 held-out target rows and retaining Devanagari Sanskrit as an independent class under strict BCP-47 tags ([Lang]-[Script]), we provide the first contamination-free open-world testbed.

Benchmark Scale: 16,155 rows (FLORES+), 17,023 rows (CommonLID), 15,047 rows (WiLI-2018) with strict atomic group stratification.
04
⚙️

Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting via Targeted Adaptation

We show that naive fine-tuning erodes global language representations. To resolve this, we engineered 5 targeted adaptation strategies—including Two-Stage Specialist Routing, LoRA attention rebalancing, and custom C++ label extension—which eliminate script ambiguity while maintaining 0.95–0.97 Macro-F1 globally.

Zero-Degradation Guarantee: Two-Stage Routing guarantees mathematically 0.0% degradation on all 200+ background languages.
05
🔍

The Devanagari Dialect Fragmentation Anomaly

We uncovered why OpenLID-v2 experiences a catastrophic drop on Devanagari Sanskrit (0.0350 F1) compared to fastText (0.9594 F1): OpenLID fragments probability across granular regional Indo-Aryan dialect heads (Bhojpuri, Maithili, Kashmiri), demonstrating an inherent tension between ultra-fine dialect identification and classical language preservation.

Error Breakdown: OpenLID-v2 misclassifies 68.1% of Sanskrit as Bhojpuri (bho_Deva) and 15.1% as Maithili (mai_Deva).

Linguistic Inflectional Markers Subword Diagnostic

The Sinhala script preserves unambiguous morphological suffixes:

• Pali: Dominated by the genitive singular -ssa (-ස්ස), ablative -smā (-ස්මා), and retroflex ligatures -ṭṭh- (-ට්ඨ-).
• Sanskrit: Characterized by visarga ඃ (ḥ), virama consonant finals ් (t/m), and complex 3-consonant conjuncts (-rtsv-).
• Sinhala: Characterized by front vowels ඇ (æ) / ඈ (ǣ) and prenazalized half-nasals ඟ (n̆g), ඬ (n̆d).

Architectural Takeaway for Team Reviewer Defense

Why deep models drop: BiGRU and CNN networks learn directional transition priors across full-sentence word sequences. When given an isolated single word, their unconditioned hidden state transitions collapse.

Why Naive Bayes wins: MNB computes the log-likelihood sum $\sum \log P(c_i | L)$. A single 3-gram like ස්ස_ carries near-infinite positive log-odds for Pali, allowing instantaneous classification without requiring sentential syntax.

System Engineering Innovation

5 Targeted Adaptation Strategies

How we prevented catastrophic forgetting of global languages while achieving 0.99 target precision.

Strategy 1 • Modular

Two-Stage Specialist Routing

A frozen global model (fastText LID-176 / OpenLID) screens incoming text. When Sinhala script is detected, it is diverted to an isolated Stage 2 specialist model. Guarantees 0.0% degradation on all non-Sinhala languages.

Strategy 2 • PEFT

LoRA + Background Rebalancing

Injected rank $r=8$, $\alpha=16$ adapter matrices into XLM-R's $Q, K, V$ attention projections. Fine-tuning batches were dynamically seeded with background languages to prevent representation drift.

Strategy 3 • C++ Patch

NLLB Weight-Preserving Head Extension

Authored a custom C++ compilation patch (extend-labels) expanding NLLB's projection matrix from 218 to 220 rows. Pre-trained weights were locked, while new vectors were trained via continuation decay.

Strategy 4 • Structural

In-Place Xavier Extension (ConLID)

Expanded ConLID's linear classification head, locking existing weight rows and initializing new target logits with Xavier uniform initialization, optimized via AdamW with validation early-stopping.

Strategy 5 • Transfer

Pretrained Vector Transfer (GlotLID)

Extracted subword embeddings from GlotLID's 2,100-language foundation space to seed target heads, pairing conservative learning rates ($lr=0.005$) to prevent weight corruption.

Linguistic Discovery

The Devanagari Dialect Fragmentation Anomaly

Why OpenLID-v2 scored 0.0350 F1 on Devanagari Sanskrit while fastText achieved 0.9594 F1.

OpenLID-v2 (Ultra-Granular Dialect Heads) 0.0350 F1

OpenLID-v2 was trained with hundreds of fine-grained South Asian regional dialect heads. When presented with classical Devanagari Sanskrit:

• 68.1% misclassified as Bhojpuri (bho_Deva)
• 15.1% misclassified as Maithili (mai_Deva)
• 43.3% of Hindi text misclassified as Kashmiri (kas_Deva)

Result: The probability mass is completely fragmented across dialect variants, collapsing classical Sanskrit identification.

fastText & NLLB (Unified Classical Tags) 0.9594 F1

fastText LID-176 and NLLB LID-218 employ unified classical language heads (sa / san_Deva):

• Concentrates full probability mass on standard classical literature.
• Avoids dialect competition on shared Indo-Aryan morphological roots.
• Reaches 0.9594 F1 on FLORES+ and 0.9904 F1 on WiLI-2018.

Core Finding: For classical literary languages, ultra-granular dialect heads actively degrade identification accuracy.

Engineering Playbook

Practical Deployment Recommendations

Actionable decision rules for digital humanities, search engines, and web-scale pipelines.

1. Historical Manuscript OCR & Search Queries
Deploy: Multinomial Naive Bayes / fastText
For isolated words or short phrases under contextual deprivation, massive neural transformers are inappropriate. MNB delivers 0.8584 F1 on single words and 0.9785 F1 on WiLI-2018 with near-zero latency and minimal CPU requirements.
2. Web-Scale Crawling (Common Crawl / WET)
Deploy: Two-Stage Specialist Routing
For industrial-scale filtering where thousands of background languages must remain undisturbed, Two-Stage Routing provides mathematical certainty of 0.0% background degradation with sub-millisecond routing overhead.
3. High-Precision Machine Translation Pipelines
Deploy: NLLB LID-218 with Head Extension
NLLB LID-218 with C++ extend-labels achieves the highest overall multi-benchmark balance (0.9530 FLORES+, 0.9675 WiLI, 0.9501 CommonLID), making it the optimal pre-filtering engine for multilingual MT.
