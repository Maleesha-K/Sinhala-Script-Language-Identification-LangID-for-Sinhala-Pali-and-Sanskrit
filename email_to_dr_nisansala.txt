KEY FLOW & SECTION CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

1. Phase 1: Closed-World Baselines & Morphological Resilience (Sections 3 & 4)
   * Full Sentences Saturate (>99.8% Macro-F1): When syntactic context is 
     available, all architectures reliably separate Sinhala, Pali, and Sanskrit.
   * 1-Word Stress Testing Divergence: Under extreme brevity (OCR fragments / 
     isolated roots), shallow subword models (Multinomial Naive Bayes at 85.8% 
     and fastText at 80.3%) significantly outperform deep neural networks 
     (Char-CNN 76.5%, BiGRU 69.2%) and margin classifiers (SVM 61.0%).
   * Linguistic Grounding: Probabilistic n-gram models directly capture diagnostic 
     inflectional suffixes (e.g., Pali genitive -ssa, Sanskrit conjuncts/visarga, 
     and Sinhala æ/ǣ vowel signs) without requiring sentence-level syntax.

2. Phase 2: Open-World Benchmarking & Adaptation (Section 5)
   * Zero-Shot Foundation Collapse: Planetary-scale foundation models (NLLB, 
     GlotLID, OpenLID-v2, fastText-176) achieve 0.0% recall on Pali-Sinh and 
     San-Sinh due to the Orthographic Fallacy (mapping script bijectively to language).
   * Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting: Unconstrained fine-tuning erodes 
     pre-trained global representations. We engineered and validated five targeted 
     adaptation strategies—including Two-Stage Specialist Routing (guaranteeing 
     mathematical 0.0% background degradation), LoRA with background replay, and a 
     custom C++ weight-preserving head extension (expanding NLLB to 220 labels).
   * Global Performance vs. Efficiency Trade-off: 
     - Global Web Resilience: Adapted foundation models achieve peak scores on 
       diverse web data (NLLB: 0.9530 on FLORES+; XLM-R: 0.9737 and fastText 
       2-Stage: 0.9645 on CommonLID) due to multi-billion-token global pre-training.
     - Baseline Efficiency: Simple from-scratch n-gram baselines remain exceptionally 
       competitive (Multinomial NB leads WiLI-2018 at 0.9785), providing near-SOTA 
       performance at near-zero compute.

3. Discussion: The Dialect Continuum Problem (Section 6)
   * We uncover that OpenLID-v2 collapses on Devanagari Sanskrit (0.0350 F1) 
     because its ultra-granular South Asian dialect heads fracture probability mass 
     across descended varieties (misclassifying 68.1% of Sanskrit as Bhojpuri and 
     15.1% as Maithili). This demonstrates that for classical literary languages, 
     unified linguistic heads are essential.

4. Provenance & Reproducibility (Appendix)
   * Complete per-class breakdown tables and dataset provenance (N = 74,318 
     target sentences across SiPaKosa, SiDiaC, SansinNT, and DCS) have been 
     balanced and verified with public access links.
